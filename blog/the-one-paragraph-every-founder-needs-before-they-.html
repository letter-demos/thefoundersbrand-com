The One Paragraph Every Founder Needs Before They Talk to Anyone

We call it the positioning paragraph, and it is the single most important piece of writing most early-stage founders do not have. It is not a tagline, though a good tagline usually comes from it. It is not an elevator pitch, though a good elevator pitch is built on top of it. It is a one-paragraph answer to the question that every investor, customer, and future hire is silently asking when they encounter your company: what is this, who is it for, and why does it exist.

The structure we recommend is straightforward. Start with the category your company lives in, so the reader has a mental shelf to put you on. Follow that with the specific person or business you serve and the problem they have. Then explain what you do differently from what already exists, and close with the outcome your customer can expect. That is four sentences. It should not be longer than four sentences.

The reason most founders struggle with this is that they know too much about their own company. They want to explain the technology, the roadmap, the nuances of the market. The positioning paragraph is not the place for any of that. It is for the reader, not the founder. It should land immediately on first read, with no prior context.

Once you have a positioning paragraph that holds up — meaning you can read it to someone who has never heard of your company and they can explain it back to you accurately — everything else gets easier. Your pitch deck has a spine. Your homepage has a direction. Your team has a shared language for what you are building. The paragraph is not the brand, but it is the seed that the brand grows from.

We work on this paragraph in the first session of almost every engagement we take. It is unglamorous work. It involves a lot of crossed-out sentences and uncomfortable questions about assumptions founders have been carrying around since they started the company. But founders who come out the other side with a sharp positioning paragraph consistently tell us it was the most useful hour they spent on their brand.
